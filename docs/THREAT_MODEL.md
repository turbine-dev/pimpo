# Threat model

Pimpo acts for people: it reads their email, sends messages and changes things. This page says what it defends against, how, and what it does not promise. Each defense has a test that fails if it breaks.

## Assets

- Accounts: email, calendar, chats, services with their tokens.
- The house's data: memory, receipts, routines.
- The owner's intent: rules, approvals and budget.

## Attackers

| Attacker | Can |
|---|---|
| A stranger who writes to you | put text in an email, page, calendar invite or photo that Pimpo reads |
| A malicious routine or connector author | publish to the gallery, or offer a connector |
| Someone on your network | reach ports on your machine |
| Another person in the house | use Pimpo with their own role |
| A thief with your phone | open the paired app |

## Defenses

| Threat | Defense | Test |

| Prompt injection in content read | External content is data. Irreversible actions always need approval. Explorations only simulate changes. | `TestGateInjectionLeaksNoSecret` |
| Poisoned memory | Anything the agent notes is low trust and never reaches later instructions. Facts are single lines, and topics cannot write outside the memory. | `TestGatePoisonedMemoryNeverBecomesInstruction` (50 attacks) |
| A fact hidden from its source | Pimpo records where every fact came from (the conversation, task, routine, job, email, import or person that gave it), not the agent: the agent may add the email it read a fact in, and that is kept only when the sender appears in mail the run read, next to the run's own source. Forgetting a source removes every fact that came even partly from it; a low-trust copy of what the person typed never attaches a source that could remove it. Sources and forgetting are per person: only a fact's author sees its source's details and forgets it, and the history shows others' forgetting only as whose it was. | `TestNotedFactsKeepTheirSources`, `TestSourcesArePrivate`, `TestForgetSourceRemovesExactlyItsFacts`, `TestEveryFactKeepsItsSources` |
| A destructive request after context was lost | Rules live outside the model, and deletes go to the trash. | `TestGateTwoHundredEmails` |
| Runaway cost or loops | The budget is checked before every model call, with call limits per run. | `TestGateLoopStopsAtBudget` |
| One person running up the house's bill, or using models they were not given | Every cost is recorded for the person it was for (chats, routines, jobs, judgments and written text), and a paid call is refused before it starts when it could pass that person's own daily limit or the house's; a guest has a small limit by default. | iMessage and the unofficial WhatsApp | Both answer only the paired owner. iMessage reads Messages' database read-only, only iMessages (never SMS, whose sender is easy to fake) that arrive after it starts, and sends with the text as an argument, never inside the script. The unofficial WhatsApp starts only when the owner chose it in Labs and never approves: numbered answers are ignored and notices say to choose elsewhere. | `TestIMessageHandsOverNewMessagesOnly`, `TestUnofficialWhatsAppNeverApproves`, `TestUnofficialWhatsAppWaitsForLabs` |
| Long jobs | A job runs only after the owner sees the plan and starts it, within a budget the owner sets (at most $20) and the day's limit. Each part is an exploration limited to the capabilities the plan gave it (unknown ones are dropped, none means none), so it reads for real and writes only as proposals. The parts' results reach the report as data. | `TestJobPlansRunsPartsAndReports`, `TestJobStopsAtItsBudget`, `TestJobResumesAfterARestart` |
| Voice conversation | The microphone is open only while the owner has the conversation on, and speech is transcribed on the owner's Pimpo (Whisper), never by the browser's cloud recognition. A spoken answer is a new message and never an approval: proposed actions wait for a tap, so someone talking near the device, or audio played to it, cannot approve anything. | `ui/src/test/conversation.test.ts` |
| The phone as a node | Each paired phone reports only what it shares, chosen on the phone itself; a report from a phone that does not share it is refused. Automations use a separate key that can report events and nothing else, and each phone's key and login are revoked with the phone. Positions are compared with the owner's places and dropped; photos stay on the computer and their text reaches routines as data, never as instructions. | `TestPhoneArrivingHomeAndAPhotoOfABill` |
| Learned preferences | Learned only from the owner's own requests and decisions, never from content the agent read (nor from suggestions, whose titles come from email metadata); shown to the agent as learned and not confirmed, so they shape answers but never pass a rule or approve an action; the owner confirms or removes each, and a removed one is never learned again. | `TestLearnsOnlyFromTheOwnersOwnWords` |
| Lessons (what Pimpo proposes to keep) | A lesson is only a proposal: learned preferences, notes the agent took (low trust, maybe read in an email), repeated tasks, repairs and suggestions are shown with their evidence and change nothing until the person accepts; accepting goes through the usual path (confirming the fact, compiling the exploration with its checks and audit, keeping the old version in the history), so a lesson never bypasses rules or approvals. Rejecting is remembered by a fingerprint of what it would keep and the lesson is never proposed or learned again. Lessons are per person: each sees and decides only their own, the owner included; suggestions are the owner's only. The weekly digest carries a count and a link, never buttons or numbers to answer, so a channel that can be taken over cannot accept a lesson. | `TestLessonsAreProposedNeverApplied`, `TestAcceptEditAndRejectLessons`, `TestRejectingARepairDiscardsIt`, `TestLessonsArePerPerson`, `TestTheWeeklyDigestOnlyLinks` |
| A product owner's briefs misleading the CEO | Every claim in a brief cites a source and quotes it, and Jev, reading the quote as data, flags claims it does not support and those that quote nothing; the flags stay on the brief. Briefs only propose: a person accepts, rejects or marks them shipped. A shipped brief is checked against its own predictions on days 30 and 90, only by its author or the work asked to, and its author's accuracy is shown. Signals and their text reach members as data. | `TestAProductOwnersBriefIsCheckedAfterItShips`, `TestABriefScoresAsTheRoadmapSays`, `TestTheSameSignalCountsOnce` |
| A company member's coding CLI on the host (`code.workspace`) | Only a company member's live work calls it, under the house's and the company's rules like any other action. The CLI (Claude Code, Codex or opencode, chosen per member) works in a git worktree of its own per task, under the company's folder, on a branch of its own; it gets its own file and command tools there and no Pimpo tools, no web search or fetch, none of the repository's own Claude Code settings or hooks, an environment with only what it needs to run and sign in plus the company's coding variables (no vault secret, no git config, so none of the person's git credentials), and a cost cap of at most $3 per call. Pimpo commits with the member's name, hooks and signing off, and pushes with the member's GitHub token, given to git as a header for that command only. Merging stays `github.merge`, irreversible. | Code the agent runs (`code.run`) | Off until the owner turns it on; a fresh Docker container per call with no network, a read-only root, all Linux capabilities dropped, no-new-privileges, an unprivileged user, no environment, and limits on time, memory, CPU, processes and output, from official images pinned by digest ([RFC 0001](rfcs/0001-code-sandbox.md)). | `TestEveryRunIsIsolated`, `TestImagesArePinnedByDigest`, `TestCodeRunsOnlyWhenTurnedOn`, `TestLive` (with Docker) |
| A malicious skill (SKILL.md) | A skill is third-party text the agent loads as reference, never as the owner's words; once loaded, the rest of that exploration reaches only the capabilities the owner granted it (none means none), its scripts are never run, the protection list refuses known ones by hash (at preview and again every time one is loaded), and a skill changed on disk stops working. | `TestInstallASkill`, `TestProtectionListRefusesASkill` |
| A crafted email subject steering suggestions | Suggestions see metadata only, told it is data; they never act: accepting one shows the request and starts an ordinary exploration, which simulates changes. Each round is capped at $0.02 within the daily limit. | `TestSuggestionsFromMetadataOnly` |
| Leaking secrets | Secrets sit in an encrypted vault whose key is in the OS keychain. Models see capabilities, never credentials. Backups are sealed as a whole with a passphrase of 12+ characters (scrypt N=2^17); staged imports keep their secrets encrypted with the vault key until they are in the vault. A vault key that cannot be read is an error, never replaced by a new one over existing secrets. A secret kept in 1Password or HashiCorp Vault is stored as a reference and read only when a connector uses it, held in memory for five minutes and never written to disk; the op CLI runs with only `PATH`, `HOME` and its token, and a time limit; password manager addresses must be https (plain http only to this computer, for the house) and redirects are not followed. A person's references resolve only with that person's own credentials, never the house's, and the house's 1Password app is for the house alone. Checking a reference answers only whether it was found; errors name the reference, never a value; backups carry references, not values. | `TestExportImportRoundTrip`, `TestStagedImportKeepsSecretsUntilTheyAreInTheVault`, `TestOSKeyOnlyCreatesForAnEmptyVault`, `TestFileKeyIsNeverReplaced`, `TestOnePasswordReferencesResolveWithACleanEnvironment`, `TestAReferenceThatFailsNamesTheReference`, `TestAPersonsReferencesUseOnlyTheirOwnCredentials`, `TestHashiCorpVaultReferences`, `TestPasswordManagersMustBeReachedOverHTTPS`, `TestOnePasswordConnectReferences`, `TestExportKeepsReferences`, `TestPasswordManagersArePerPersonAndNeverShowSecrets` |
| A key typed where it does not belong | When a connector lacks a key or the service refuses it (HTTP 401), Pimpo's code, never a model, opens a request for the person the run acts for, naming only the connector and field; the notice on every channel carries only a link to a form in the app, which only that person can open or answer (the administrator included gets 404 for a member's). The form writes straight to the vault under the name Pimpo chose for that person's connector; the answer, the event and what the run and the model read back say only that it was saved, and the link works once and for 7 days. A house key (an external connector's) is asked only of the administrator; guests get no requests. Keys pasted into the app's chat, a new task or any channel message (Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, Slack, Signal, email) are taken out by a detector of common formats and of values written after words like "password:" before the text reaches the model, the conversation, the store or the log; the person is warned and a message that was only a key goes no further. It redacts and never blocks. Limit: a key in a format it does not know, typed without a label, goes through. | `TestAMissingKeyIsAskedForPrivately`, `TestAnExplorationAsksAndTheModelNeverSeesTheValue`, `TestCredentialRequestsBelongToTheirPerson`, `TestPastedKeysAreRemovedBeforeAnythingSeesThem`, `TestMissingAndRefusedKeys`, `TestRedactsKnownFormats`, `TestLeavesOrdinaryText` |
| A routine doing more than it says | The runtime exposes only declared capabilities. The gallery signs content and audits each routine with every capability reachable. | A changed release or update | Each release's `checksums.txt` is signed with the release key (`checksums.txt.sig`); `pimpo update` checks the signature against the release public keys built into the binary before trusting any checksum, and refuses to install when the signature is missing or does not match. `scripts/install.sh` does the same when its `openssl` can verify Ed25519 (OpenSSL 3), and otherwise warns and checks the checksum only. | A connector doing more than it says | It runs as a separate process with a clean environment, exactly its declared tools, timeouts, and risk-based rules. | Known exfiltration endpoints and patterns | A signed protection list is checked before every action, including other agents' actions through the Guard. | Widgets and dashboards | `widget.show` only reaches the routine's own person; a snapshot has fixed kinds, capped sizes and https links only, and is always drawn as text. | Companies of agents | Off until the owner turns them on in Labs. ## Not promised

- A compromised computer. Anyone who controls the machine Pimpo runs on controls Pimpo.
- A model's judgment. Judgments are probabilities; anything irreversible waits for a person.
- A person who approves something harmful. The receipts show it, and undo works where the service allows.
- Services' own security (Google, Meta, Telegram).
